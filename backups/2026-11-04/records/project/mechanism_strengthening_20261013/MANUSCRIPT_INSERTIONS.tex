\paragraph{Joint analysis of mixing and spatial weighting.}
We froze the two main-Full students and the EMA teacher and evaluated four combinations of fixed or TCSMix regions and uniform or CTW weights on the ACDC 10\% validation set. The 210 distinct slices from 10 patients formed nine predeclared batches with complementary mixtures and shared masks across aligned weak and strong views; six repeated source exposures completed the final batch. Uniform weights preserved each image's mean CTW weight. We differentiated the original $0.05\,\mathrm{mean}(w e)$ consistency term with respect to student-1 logits while detaching student 2 and the weights, and summarized source-patient statistics with equal patient weights. Ground truth defined offline anatomical and error regions only.
The teacher confidence ranged from 0.996357 to 0.997640, and every dynamic rectangle rounded to 97 by 97 pixels. Consequently, the fixed and dynamic conditions produced identical mixtures and cannot establish an interaction benefit on these frozen inputs. CTW increased the anatomical-boundary share of the consistency gradient from 46.60\% to 56.02\%, while the mean boundary gradient increased by a factor of 2.23. The mean weight was 0.476 on erroneous pseudo-label source pixels and 0.255 on correct source pixels. These results locate CTW's emphasis at confidence transitions, including difficult anatomy and pseudo-label errors. Mixing seams and cutout responses are reported separately. Switching weights on a fixed checkpoint changes the consistency loss and gradient while preserving its predictions.

\paragraph{Residual intervention in TRC.}
We compared normal inference with a temporary $\gamma=0$ intervention in the paper's main-Full student 1, using the same checkpoint, validation images, and original inference protocol. The learned gate was $\gamma=0.035988$. Restoring the gate reproduced the normal logits bitwise on all 210 slices, and the checkpoint remained unchanged.
Normal TRC changed 86 of 13,762,560 pixels on the 256-grid diagnostic images relative to the intervention, correcting 46 errors and introducing 36; 4 changed pixels were incorrect in both conditions. Patient-equal native-volume Dice was 89.99081\% with normal TRC and 89.98905\% with $\gamma=0$, a difference of 0.00176 percentage points. The small response indicates limited immediate dependence on the residual in this trained checkpoint. The existing training ablation reports 90.0894\% test Dice without TRC and 90.3497\% for Full with TCSMix and CTW retained. These observations distinguish the benefit of training with TRC from the residual's instantaneous contribution at the selected checkpoint; the intervention uses validation data, while the existing ablation uses test data.

\paragraph{Fixed-region training control.}
We selected a single 97-by-97 rectangle from 150 existing main-Full training records sampled every 200 updates. All recorded, rounded rectangles had area $9409/65536$, so the fixed size matches these observed endpoints. The records do not establish the mean area over all 30,000 updates. The new run reuses the main-Full pretrained weights, inherited SGD state, split, augmentations, TRC, CTW, EMA, losses, validation selection, and training budget; only the region dimensions are replaced.
The fixed control completed 30,000 updates per student. Student 1 was selected at update 29000 using the original validation criterion and achieved 89.8548\% test Dice, compared with the retained main-Full value of 90.3497\%, a difference of -0.4948 percentage points. Its HD95 and ASD were 2.2513 and 0.6998 grid units. This is a single-seed, run-level comparison of the size rules. The fixed run's own teacher signals would yield 97-by-97 rectangles at 29,999 updates and 96-by-96 once under the original rounding rule; these logged signals do not reconstruct the original Full training trajectory.

We formulate mixed-sample construction and spatial consistency allocation as linked stages of semi-supervised segmentation. TCSMix controls the construction of complementary mixed samples, CTW controls their spatial learning contribution, and TRC conditions decoder features through a learned residual. Our frozen-model diagnostics identify where the original consistency term allocates real logit gradients and quantify the trained model's dependence on the TRC residual.
